---
name: mechanism-animation
description: Turn a validated multi-part CAD model into animated visualisations whose parts drive each other, that obey the stated kinematics, and that never pass through one another. Covers the motion definition, baking, GLB export, viewer contract, exploded views and the checks that make the motion trustworthy.
---

Read `cad_pipeline/README.md` ("Wright revision 2 in the Explore gallery") and [references/pipeline.md](references/pipeline.md). Start only from a model that passed cad-validation's static rungs and whose research has a motion dossier and packaging record (engineering-research, `references/motion-and-packaging.md`). Presentation-only camera tours stay in cad-to-blender; this skill is for motion that claims to show operation.

**One definition.** Write the motion once in a standard-library module (`cad_pipeline/wright_motion.py`): `matrix(part_id, theta)` returns each part's 4x4 transform at crank angle theta, built from the declared axes, ratios, stroke, lift and phases, with rigid bodies from `wright_bodies.py`. The development baker, the Blender rig, the tests and the audit tool all import it, so they cannot disagree. Do not key values by hand in Blender.

**Drive followers from drivers.** The animation should be a chain of causes, not independent loops: crank to rods and pistons by the slider-crank, crank to cam by the chain ratio, cam profile to rocker angle to valve lift to spring length, ignition cam to trip lever to igniter lever, flywheel rim to friction wheel by the no-slip radius ratio. Solve each follower from its driver's surface with a clearance margin, and let automatic parts (suction valves) follow the physics that moves them. Springs that compress change length (scale along their axis); they do not slide.

**Declare what is source, derived or illustrative,** in the motion note, the contract scope text (shown with every motion) and the interface. Ratios, stroke, lift and the drive paths come from sources; firing order, phase windows, cam lobe shape and inlet lift are teaching choices unless the sources give them. Parts that are not animated (a deferred oil pump) are listed with the reason.

**Bake for interpolation.** One key per crank degree, linear, two crank turns for a four-stroke cycle. A viewer interpolates between keys, so a part that turns about a fixed axis has its node origin on that axis (`pivot()`), and followers keep clearance margin for the jumps between keys. Per-body tracks (all parts of one rigid body share one set of keys), channels that never change are left out, each clip is kept as a muted NLA track so the glTF exporter writes it, and the contract is built from the same research inventory the tree and descriptions use.

**Exploded views are motion too.** A systems exploded view moves whole systems in stages and is pedagogical, but a student can pause on every stage and watch the transit. Plan a removal order and direction for each system so parts do not cross each other, using the audit as the oracle (`scripts/scan_assembly_pairs.py --fractions`, `--union`). The Wright view is straight-line staged motion and its transit and held poses still overlap (14 and 18 pairs at the ends of stages 2 and 3); say so wherever you ship one that does.

**Check before you ship.** Test the exported GLB by loading it as a viewer does (three.js `AnimationMixer`): drive ratios, stroke against the slider-crank formula, valve lift and where it falls in the stroke, fixed axes not drifting, exploded stages and rest pose, clips switching back to the assembled pose. Audit the baked clip between keys (cad-validation ladder, rung 5), open the model in the app, step each stage and play each clip. Nothing here establishes dynamics, forces, inertia or gas pressure; do not label it a simulation.
