# Wright engine: research before the second reconstruction

Reviewed 4 October 2026. Target: a detailed educational reconstruction of the **surviving rebuilt horizontal four-cylinder assembly**, using the existing museum-derived exterior mesh, the Science Museum construction illustrated by Hobbs, and documented 1903 nominal cylinder dimensions. This is a source-led CAD study, not a certified manufacturing reproduction of the engine that flew on 17 December 1903.

## What was wrong with the first pass

The first pass researched a few nominal dimensions and system summaries, then generated a plausible engine envelope. Its checks proved CAD solidity and export consistency, but did not establish component completeness, correct construction or meaningful mechanism interfaces. Bare camshafts, annular spring envelopes, circular valve-cage placeholders, solid timing disks and an unspecified pump concealed the missing understanding. Its broad provenance labels could not make those omissions acceptable. The first model is retained for comparison, not used as the design authority for this revision.

The new order is: understand the selected configuration; inspect sections, photographs and figure keys; inventory components and trace mechanisms; decide disputed construction; review dimensions and gaps; plan features and validation; generate; compare back to sources. `inventory.json` covers every number 1–47 in Hobbs Figure 5 plus additional components from the prose/sections. `research_gate.py` checks that records are complete and actually map to the chosen parts. Human source interpretation remains necessary.

## Configuration and source decisions

H1 pp. 5–8 explains that no complete original 1903 engine drawing survives. The Science Museum measured the assembled engine in 1928 and corrected/expanded the drawings during disassembly in 1939. The Christman set began in late 1947 with Orville's involvement. H1 explicitly finds the two sets incompatible in dimensions, construction and firing order. The original crankcase broke; the original shaft and flywheel were lent and lost. H1 identifies the surviving engine's crankshaft and flywheel as taken from the 1904–1905 flight engine. It records reassemblies in 1926 and 1928. These facts prevent an exact-original claim based on the museum mesh.

The revised model therefore follows the **surviving/Science Museum construction** where H1 identifies a conflict, while retaining the documented original nominal 4 × 4-inch cylinder geometry. The connecting rod uses the three-piece directly threaded steel tube and bronze-end construction illustrated by the Science Museum section, not the Smithsonian adapter arrangement. Actual thread pitch and fit remain unresolved and are represented by engagement lands, with pins shown separately. Bronze rod bearings are modelled directly; the disputed claim of babbitted rod ends is not adopted. Main/cam bearings use babbitt categories.

The oil pump is a special case. H1 p. 28 says the surviving engine has a gear pump driven from the **crankshaft by worm and cross shaft**, although Figure 5's key describes a camshaft gear drive. H1 questions whether a pump was present in 1903 and notes that Christman drawings omit it. This revision labels the pump **rebuilt-engine accessory**. The narrative's crankshaft/cross-shaft route is the research direction, but **placed drive geometry is deferred** until the chosen-lineage drawing establishes its route and dimensions. An attempted generic cross shaft would have passed through the water jacket; a source conflict cannot justify such a placement. The pump has an illustrative gear pair, casing and connections; exact worm profile and pump dimensions are not established. Figure 5 callout 14 records this deliberate omission.

Later horizontal engines added a larger bore, auxiliary barrel exhaust ports, fuel pumping and compression release. Later vertical engines changed the casting/jacket arrangement and rod material, and later six-cylinder engines used different cylinders and rods. Those changes are excluded. There is no mechanical inlet cam, modern carburetor float bowl, modern high-tension spark plug, barrel exhaust-port ring or compression-release shaft in this assembly.

## Operating and construction understanding

**Load and motion.** Pressure acts on a long cast-iron piston. The pin is locked in the piston by a setscrew; a split bronze little end also permits clamping, though H1 found no example where the pin was held there instead. The seamless tube and directly threaded bronze ends transmit reversing load to the split big-end bearing and crankpin. Big-end bolts, shims and locking strips matter because they close the bearing and maintain alignment. The four-throw high-carbon-steel crankshaft has plain journals, cheeks and no counterweights. Five main bearing stations transfer loads into end walls and intermediate ribs of the one-piece casting. A cast-iron flywheel, keyed and shrunk onto the shaft, moderates torque into the external propeller drives. Aircraft propeller chains are outside this cropped engine assembly; engine-side drive sprockets are included.

**Cylinder assembly and sealing.** The aluminum/copper-alloy casting combines the case and water jacket. A thin steel upper cover closes the crank space. Bearing bosses split at 45 degrees facilitate assembly. Each short cast-iron liner has an integral restricted head/connection boss, is threaded into the case at two regions and into a transverse valve box. A head-end gasket bears against both the case and liner flange. This is a water-and-gas sealing problem involving three joints and two thread locations, not four unrelated tubes inside a generic box. The liner's upper oil holes must align after tightening. The piston projects substantially out of the short liner at the crank end, but its three pinned compression rings are all above the wrist pin.

**Gas path.** Gravity fuel enters an open-ended beaded air can and flows with air through a shallow hot-plate induction chamber. Integral casting ribs/baffles force the stream to sweep the exposed hot jacket wall before entering the separate low sheet-steel intake manifold. Each automatic inlet valve opens from suction against its spring, with no positive inlet drive. Both valves have cast-iron heads, separate steel stems and cage seats/guides. Each cage is an open four-legged construction held against a shoulder by an internal ring nut. Exhaust travels through the lower cage passages to a row of apertures in the valve-box wall. The valve boxes are uncooled; adding a jacket around them would contradict the source.

**Exhaust timing.** The crank sprocket drives a bicycle chain onto a double-tooth-count cam sprocket. A hardwood block tensions the chain. The hollow mild-steel exhaust camshaft is carried in three plain bearing lugs; separate cams and locating washers are sweated on. Each rocker consists of two steel cheeks, a pivot axle and a roller at each end. A cam roller receives motion and the valve-end roller opens the exhaust stem. The cam profile sought rapid opening and dwell, but this revision cannot recover an exact acceleration law. Lobe shapes/poses are illustrative and no running animation is certified.

**Ignition and control.** The exhaust shaft drives an equal-size spur gear on the solid ignition shaft in the opposite direction. Its gear/sleeve slides axially and drives a shaft pin in a 45-degree slot; a lever cam and opposing spring set relative phase. Four bent-strip ignition cams act on paired external levers and main/inter-springs, transmitting motion through a sealed oscillating shaft into each chamber. The movable platinum-faced contact closes against an insulated fixed electrode, then snaps open. Generator feed reaches all four fixed electrodes through a busbar with individual connections; the return travels through the contact/engine ground. This is make-and-break ignition, not four ordinary spark plugs. The purchased generator needs visible horseshoe magnet, coil bodies, armature/shaft, mounting base, friction wheel and wiring. The friction wheel touches the flywheel rim. Internal winding/pole design remains an envelope; dry cells and starting coil are ground equipment and excluded.

**Oil and coolant.** Bearing lubrication is splash/drip, not modern pressure feed to every bearing. In the selected rebuilt configuration, sump compartments communicate with a return gallery; a pump return/feed leads to a distributor pipe with four jets at the upper thrust face of the liners. Big-end scuppers participate in splash. The generator has a sight-feed lubricator. Hollow water-jacket space surrounds the barrels, with one lower inlet and two upper returns, connected in service to an airframe radiator. No water pump is fitted. Engine-side pipes are included; a fabricated full radiator/tank installation would require separate airframe references.

**Assembly order.** Machined casting and bearing ribs/bosses receive bearing shells/caps and crankshaft through the open upper/end access; rods/pistons and short liners are fitted with aligned pin/rod ends and oil holes; liner/box/gasket joints are tightened; cages, heads/stems, ring nuts and springs are installed; shafts, separate cams, rockers, chain and tensioner are assembled and timed; ignition gear/control/contact mechanisms and busbar are installed; sheet induction/manifold and cover plates close the assembly; generator, oil accessory and external service connections are attached. This order explains the access openings, split bearings and detachable plates.

Figure 4's small reproduction carries overall dimensions approximately 32.425, 28.844 and 15.25 inches. They are recorded as legibility-limited envelope checks, not reliable manufacturing constraints. The mesh's full bounds cannot be compared to them because its long curved service tubes and extent definitions differ. A few valve-box fits support approximate repeated geometry only; they do not certify global scan/CAD agreement.

The imported-to-CAD candidate transform remains `mm = 10 * (mesh - [-46, 1.2, -21])`, XYZ with cylinders along +X, crankshaft along +Y, Z upward. Scale is a documented **inference**, not a measured fact. The revised model uses the repeated housing/pitch observations as guide geometry and independently documented interior dimensions. Nominal cylinder dimensions cannot directly calibrate a scan that hides the bore. Source overlay checks will report individual exterior deviations without labelling the full model a recovered scan.

Exact casting contours, rod/journal sizes, thread pitches, tolerances, cam laws, crank/ignition phases, valve-seat included angle for this particular drawing lineage, spring rates/turn counts, gasket material, magneto internals, oil-pump dimensions and full service-line routing remain unresolved. These are bounded, separately labelled teaching choices. They are not made 'known' by a successful build. The valve-seat angle range 90–110 degrees in H1 spans engines; 90 degrees in this revision is an explicit teaching estimate.

## Readiness decision and validation plan

The information now supports a substantially more complete static mechanism/assembly study: component roles, materials, connection topology, principal nominal dimensions and variant choices have source locators. It does **not** support exact first-flight manufacture or an authenticated operating animation. The oil-pump drive remains deferred; uncertain profiles, fits and accessory sizes remain estimates. The cropped model excludes two inventoried airframe-service items explicitly.

Before generation, cover all 47 Figure 5 callouts and supplemental components; account explicitly for the pump-drive conflict, rod-joint lineage, chain tensioner and excluded later-engine parts. After generation, independently reopen all CAD solids and STEP, verify bore/wall/valve dimensions, pin and journal alignments, rocker/cam/valve contacts, gear-center/radius relationships, cage windows, spring clearance, friction-drive contact, water/gas paths and meaningful parameter propagation. Measure chain spacing and report it honestly: the equal-arc-length static layout has unequal curved-span chord spacing and **does not pass an operating chain/pitch validation**. Produce named overview, cutaway, subsystem and component-detail views in Blender. Derive the cylinder section from native CAD rather than inventing an illustration. Compare those views to Figures 1, 2, 5, 6 and 7; record discrepancies and repair them. Coverage, geometry, mechanism checks and source agreement are separate report categories.

During assembly review, the lower coolant feed was moved between liners, the liner flange received a cast counterbore, main journals received through-bores and upper cap access, and the hot-plate/manifold received communicating openings and spring/guide clearance. Rocker cheek clearance and valve-roller tangency were corrected. These changes address concrete source interfaces; they do not establish authentic manufacturing fits.
