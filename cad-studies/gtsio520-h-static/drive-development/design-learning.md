# Drive reconstruction lessons

Transfer the reasoning and tested methods to the next engine, not these estimated dimensions.

1. Separate torque, bearing, thrust and oil paths. The long quill transmits crank torque; the propeller oil-transfer tube does not. Model each connection and its capture independently. Bearing clearance alone cannot establish propeller endplay: include both thrust washers and the shaft shoulders in the measured stack.
2. Derive a single gear declaration. Ratio alone does not specify tooth count, module, width or OEM timing. The shared declarations supply pitch centres, phase and rate; calculated involute outlines use source-backed backlash with explicit estimated tooth geometry. A complete sampled tooth-pitch test detects a deliberately wrong phase. Static clearance and this analytical fixture are separate from future exported animation validation.
3. Check the whole hardware envelope. A 14 mm bolt-row pitch looked plausible until 17 mm nuts overlapped. R2 uses 24 mm row spacing. A shaft bore must cross the whole case width, and its flat land must fit every washer and nut. A locating pin must remain inside the washer thickness; shrinking its envelope resolved contact with the rotating collar.
4. Keep support material around line-bored seats. A broad gear cavity can remove a bearing support despite leaving a valid case solid. Station-specific cavities and annular material probes test the actual support ring. A connected fluid probe tests openings across sleeve/tube/shaft boundaries; it establishes an open modeled passage, not pressure or hydraulic performance.
5. Test construction topology rather than patch the mesh. Three nonmanifold edges persisted through finer and coarser tessellation. A final removeSplitter trial failed the original boundary-preservation guard and was rejected. Refinement at each authoritative Boolean instead produced a closed mesh, with both Boolean boundary differences zero against the unrefined construction. The full direct export then passed all neighbouring pairs. Saved native and final exported checks remain separate.
6. Delete owned history recursively, never linked dependencies. Replacing a part with nested PartDesign dressups initially left an identified fillet behind. The backend now deletes children owned through Group before deleting their parent, while preserving externally linked masters. A native fixture proves that distinction. The old incomplete checkpoint and a hash-bound recovered checkpoint preserve the diagnosis.
7. Reuse verified geometry conservatively. Cache adoption follows unchanged controlling parameters and transitive master dependencies, records BREP hashes, and rejects changed base files. It accelerates direct evaluation but never supplies native geometry to the independent direct checker. Every final part still receives the original saved/direct tolerances.

The complete construction checkpoint encountered a strict reused-history mismatch on RodBoltB5, also numerically sensitive in the prior valve-train phase. The current build explicitly reconstructed that same recipe rather than relaxing the guard. Its independent saved/direct and STEP outcomes must be read from the final validation records. No numerical exception is inferred from the earlier diagnosis.

The D-family attachment pattern is a selected teaching configuration. Exact H serial-number applicability, gear/spline counts, casting contours and hidden galleries remain unresolved. Fair comparison needs separate exterior and exposed/section views: an exploded gear drawing cannot calibrate an assembled case silhouette.

Final saved/STEP checks passed without a numerical exception after the explicit same-recipe RodBoltB5 rebuild. All 4,808 native neighbouring pairs and all 4,473 final GLB neighbours pass. Actual bearing/thrust/support/open-passage probes pass. This validates the bounded static construction while the visible cast shell and shaft shoulders still need refinement.

Presentation taught two independent lessons: frame the projected bounds of an asymmetric part group rather than the world bounding-box centre, and preserve the model's up direction. Correct lighting and cameras expose design differences; they cannot correct casting geometry. The preserved rejected plates, fully framed section/rear gears and unchanged mesh/GLB hashes document that distinction.

Expensive intersection work should carry forward as evidence, with explicit invalidation. Native BREP boundaries and full assembled placements provide a stricter identity than recipe, mass or bounding box. Nine saved-CAD/provenance tests show that moving a part, changing a master or changing an internal cut triggers new tests; completed unchanged pairs and partial interrupted runs can be reused, while cached collisions remain failures. Keep tolerance and software gates fixed. Transfer this method to other static engines, while keeping dynamic/exported and interface checks in their own scope.
